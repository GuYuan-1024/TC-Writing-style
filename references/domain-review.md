# Domain Review for Transportation and Low-Altitude Papers

Review as a skeptical transportation, travel-behavior, planning, or UAM reviewer. Prioritize issues that could change the paper's validity, interpretation, or decision relevance.

## 1. Story and Contribution

- Is the mobility/planning decision clear before the method?
- Does the literature genuinely establish the gap?
- Are research questions, contributions, analyses, and conclusions aligned?
- Is the paper more than a method applied to a convenient city or dataset?
- Is novelty stated as a precise difference from the closest work?

## 2. Constructs and Data

- What exactly do “demand,” “acceptance,” “accessibility,” “coverage,” “equity,” “sustainability,” and “safety” mean here?
- Do variables operationalize those constructs?
- Are sampling, missingness, spatial resolution, temporal alignment, and data provenance transparent?
- Is the study population or area appropriate for the claim?
- Are observed and assumed quantities clearly separated?

## 3. Method and Identification

- Why is this method suitable under the available data and decision setting?
- What assumptions identify the behavioral relationship or planning solution?
- Are repeated observations, spatial dependence, endogeneity, heterogeneity, and uncertainty handled or bounded?
- For integrated frameworks, are stages truly coupled and units consistent?
- For optimization, are objectives, constraints, weights, and solution selection defensible?

## 4. Empirical Credibility

- Are diagnostics, baselines, effect sizes, uncertainty, and robustness adequate?
- Do algorithm comparisons assess search performance, decision quality, or both?
- Are cross-study comparisons commensurable?
- Are sensitivity ranges plausible and tied to sources?
- Could a reasonable alternative assumption reverse the conclusion?

## 5. Transportation Meaning

- Are results translated into traveler behavior, generalized cost, accessibility, service quality, capacity, network effects, or spatial distribution?
- Are mechanisms tested or clearly labeled as interpretations?
- Are first/last-mile, multimodal, peak-period, and network feedback effects relevant and addressed?
- Does the paper discuss who gains, who is uncovered, or who bears exposure/cost?

## 6. Low-Altitude and Emerging-System Credibility

- Is the deployment horizon/scenario explicit?
- Are vehicle performance, price, modal share, regulation, noise, safety, and vertiport assumptions sourced and tested?
- Does the paper separate technological capability, modeled demand, commercial viability, regulatory feasibility, and public acceptance?
- Are claims conditional on the maturity of the system and supporting ground network?
- Is “real-world” used accurately when inputs remain simulated, transferred, or scenario-based?

## 7. Planning and Policy Use

- Which actor has authority to implement each recommendation?
- What action follows from the evidence?
- Through what mechanism should it improve the outcome?
- What trade-off, resource, legal, or institutional constraint applies?
- Does a case-specific result support a rule, a scenario, or only a local illustration?

## 8. Writing and Presentation

- Does each paragraph have a recoverable main message?
- Are contrasts and transitions logical rather than formulaic?
- Are terms, units, study years, and geographic labels consistent?
- Do figures reveal frameworks, spatial patterns, and trade-offs at readable scale?
- Do tables support comparison rather than duplicate prose?
- Are causal and generalization verbs calibrated to the design?

## Review Artifacts

For high-risk claims, create:

| Claim | Evidence | Interpretation | Boundary | Status/action |
|---|---|---|---|---|

For prospective studies, create:

| Input | Observed/estimated/calibrated/borrowed/expert/assumed | Source | Sensitivity/validation | Decision affected |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Resolve validity and claim-evidence issues before sentence polish. Report the highest-risk issues first, then section-level flow, then language and formatting.
