# Results and Discussion

## Evidence Ladder

Arrange results so each layer earns the next:

1. data/sample or spatial diagnostics;
2. descriptive patterns and baseline conditions;
3. primary model estimates or optimized alternatives;
4. magnitude, uncertainty, heterogeneity, spatial distribution, or trade-offs;
5. comparison with baselines, prior studies, or alternative specifications;
6. sensitivity, robustness, or scenario analysis;
7. behavioral, operational, planning, or policy interpretation.

Discussion may be integrated with Results or separated. In either form, keep observations distinct from explanations and recommendations.

## Result Paragraph Structure

For each major result, use:

`Finding -> quantitative evidence -> substantive interpretation -> comparison/mechanism -> decision relevance -> boundary`

Do not narrate tables row by row. Select results that answer the research questions, reveal heterogeneity, change a decision, contradict expectations, or expose uncertainty.

## Behavioral and Econometric Results

- Report sample size and model/diagnostic evidence needed to trust the estimate.
- Translate nonlinear coefficients into probabilities, marginal effects, elasticities, or other interpretable quantities.
- Discuss effect size and uncertainty, not only p-values.
- Explain reference categories and interaction effects correctly.
- Treat subgroup patterns as heterogeneity evidence, not stereotypes.
- Present mechanisms as hypotheses unless directly tested.
- Compare findings with prior transport-behavior evidence and explain plausible contextual differences.

A strong paragraph can move from an average pattern to the within-group question, use a model to test it, then explain why the difference matters for service design or travel demand management.

## Spatial and Optimization Results

- State the decision represented by each solution, not only its objective value.
- Report facility count/scale, coverage, access, capacity, cost, exposure, or other domain metrics in interpretable units.
- Use maps for spatial distribution and tables for exact trade-offs; explain what readers should notice.
- Compare efficient solutions with meaningful baselines, not only a weaker algorithm.
- Distinguish better search performance from better model validity.
- Explain why a solution excludes or deprioritizes areas and who bears the consequence.
- Show how alternative weights or assumptions change the preferred configuration.

When the unconstrained or preliminary scale differs from the final optimized scale, explain the economic, service, capacity, or social trade-off that causes the difference.

## Sensitivity and Robustness

Choose inputs because they are uncertain and decision-relevant, not simply convenient to vary. For each analysis state:

- baseline and variation range;
- rationale for the range;
- outputs monitored;
- thresholds, nonlinearities, or rank reversals;
- whether the substantive conclusion survives.

One-factor-at-a-time analysis shows local input influence but not joint uncertainty. Do not call a model broadly robust if only a few parameters were varied independently. Use multiway/scenario or probabilistic analysis when interactions could change the decision and data permit.

## From Results to Implications

Separate four levels:

1. **Observed/model result:** what the data or model produced.
2. **Interpretation:** what the pattern may mean behaviorally, spatially, or operationally.
3. **Decision implication:** what an identified actor could change.
4. **Outcome claim:** what benefit is expected and under which assumptions.

For example, a higher modeled demand for real-time information may support targeted information provision; it does not by itself prove that an app will reduce congestion. A high modeled vertiport coverage rate may support a candidate network; it does not prove public acceptance, safety approval, or commercial viability.

## Results Check

- Does every research question receive a visible answer?
- Are the most important magnitudes easy to find?
- Are statistical, behavioral, and policy importance distinguished?
- Are spatial winners, losers, and uncovered groups visible?
- Are unexpected results explained cautiously?
- Do comparisons use commensurable definitions, data, and scenarios?
- Does robustness analysis test the assumptions carrying the main claim?
