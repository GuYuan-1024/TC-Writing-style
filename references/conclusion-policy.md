# Conclusion and Policy/Planning Implications

## Conclusion Sequence

Use a compact sequence:

1. Restate the decision problem and research purpose without repeating the Introduction.
2. Answer the research questions with the most important quantitative and qualitative findings.
3. State the conceptual, empirical, methodological, or decision-support contribution actually demonstrated.
4. Translate findings into actor-specific planning, service, or policy implications.
5. State limitations as boundaries on interpretation.
6. Propose future work that directly relaxes those boundaries.

Do not make the conclusion a chronological recap of procedures.

## Implication Formula

Write major implications as:

`Actor -> action -> evidence-based mechanism -> intended outcome -> boundary/condition`

Examples of actors include travelers, mobility information providers, public transport operators, UAM operators, municipal planners, aviation regulators, communities, and infrastructure investors. Avoid treating “policymakers” as a single actor when authority is distributed.

Distinguish:

- **service design:** information bundles, timing, personalization, interfaces;
- **transport planning:** facility location, network integration, capacity, access, transfers;
- **operations:** fleet or service configuration, pricing, schedules, reliability;
- **regulation/community:** noise, safety, privacy, airspace, public engagement;
- **research/modeling:** data collection, identification, dynamic feedback, transfer testing.

## Limitations That Improve Credibility

State each limitation as:

`What is omitted or uncertain -> how it may affect the estimate/decision -> what evidence or model extension is needed`

Common transportation and low-altitude boundaries include:

- stated versus revealed behavior;
- sample coverage and self-selection;
- cross-sectional versus dynamic behavior;
- one-city context and transferability;
- temporal mismatch among GIS, socioeconomic, and travel data;
- borrowed modal shares, prices, costs, performance, or noise values;
- static capacity and peak-period operations;
- missing first/last-mile transfers or multimodal competition;
- endogenous feedback between new infrastructure, generalized cost, land use, and demand;
- unmodeled safety, airspace, acceptance, or distributional effects.

Future work should not be a wish list. Prioritize extensions that could change the central finding or decision.

## Conclusion Check

- Are the answers more prominent than the method name?
- Are numerical findings consistent with Results and Abstract?
- Are implications supported by the measured constructs and model scope?
- Are scenario results described conditionally?
- Does each limitation bound a specific claim?
- Is transfer to other cities described through required conditions rather than asserted?
