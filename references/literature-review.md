# Literature Review

## Organize Around the Decision

The review should construct the design requirements of the study. Group prior work by one or more of:

- research question or decision stage;
- behavioral mechanism or construct;
- data source and evidence type;
- modeling approach and its assumptions;
- planning factor or objective;
- spatial/temporal scale;
- empirical versus synthetic validation.

Avoid an author-by-author inventory. Within each stream, synthesize what is established, where results disagree, and what the stream cannot answer for the present decision.

## Useful Taxonomies

For travel behavior and demand:

- behavioral effects versus underlying needs or preferences;
- attitudes/acceptance versus stated or revealed choices;
- aggregate averages versus heterogeneity and segmentation;
- pre-trip versus en-route, commuting versus non-commuting, or other decision contexts;
- single information/service types versus integrated choice sets;
- survey, experiment, passive/mobile data, administrative data, or hybrid evidence.

For UAM/eVTOL demand:

- survey or stated-preference estimation;
- analogy with taxi, airport, premium, or other transport markets;
- traditional trip-generation/distribution/mode-split approaches;
- simulation, synthetic population, mobile signaling, or multisource big data;
- operational observations, when genuinely available.

For vertiport or infrastructure planning:

- factors: demand, accessibility, safety, airspace/terrain, cost, capacity, connectivity, noise, environment, equity, and acceptance;
- methods: hub/location-allocation, p-median/p-center/coverage, clustering, mixed-integer programming, heuristics/metaheuristics, MCDM, simulation, and integrated demand-location models;
- evidence: hypothetical networks, synthetic demand, transferred parameters, and real-world geographic or travel data.

## Synthesis Paragraph

End each major stream with four elements:

1. what the stream makes possible;
2. its assumption, evidence, or decision boundary;
3. why that boundary matters here;
4. the design requirement it creates for this study.

At the end of the review, synthesize a small set of linked gaps. A useful structure is:

`What is known -> What remains disconnected or weakly evidenced -> Why the disconnect changes the decision -> What the present study must therefore do`

## Gap Discipline

Distinguish these gap types:

- **Knowledge gap:** a phenomenon or relationship is unknown.
- **Measurement gap:** the construct is poorly operationalized or data are inadequate.
- **Method gap:** available methods cannot represent a necessary feature or coupling.
- **Empirical gap:** a method or claim lacks real-world testing or a relevant context.
- **Decision gap:** evidence exists but cannot guide the target actor's choice.

Do not use “few studies” as the whole argument. Explain what consequence follows from the missing evidence. Do not manufacture novelty by ignoring adjacent transport, planning, operations research, aviation, environmental, or behavioral literature.

## Review-to-Method Bridge

Before the Methods section, make the mapping explicit:

| Literature limitation | Design requirement | Study response |
|---|---|---|
| Example: demand and siting are analyzed separately | demand must affect location objectives/constraints | couple OD demand with coverage and facility decisions |
| Example: average acceptance hides heterogeneity | model population and context variation | include interpretable segment and interaction effects |
| Example: future UAM has limited observations | use transparent low-data assumptions | scenario-based forecasting plus sensitivity analysis |

Use the table as an analysis aid; it need not appear in the manuscript.
